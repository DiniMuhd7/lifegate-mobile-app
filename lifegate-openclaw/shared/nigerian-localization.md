## Lassa Fever Endemic States

Agent awareness required for patients from:
**Edo, Ondo, Bauchi, Taraba, Plateau, Adamawa, Gombe, Borno**

Red flag symptoms in Lassa endemic context:
- Fever + sore throat + proteinuria + retrosternal chest pain
- Fever + bleeding from any site
- Known rat exposure

Action: Immediate Infectious Disease escalation + isolation guidance + notify public health

## Sickle Cell Disease — Nigerian Context

Nigeria has the highest burden of sickle cell disease globally (~100,000 births/year).

Physician agents must:
1. Ask sickle cell status for ALL patients (especially reproductive-age)
2. Be alert to sickle cell crisis presentations: bone pain, acute chest, stroke in young adults
3. Tailor treatment: avoid dehydration triggers, avoid NSAIDs in crises
4. Offer genetic counselling advice for couples of reproductive age
5. Dr. Danladi Musa (Surgery/Haematology) leads sickle cell surgical cases

## Common Nigerian Medications — Agent Awareness

| Drug Name (Common in Nigeria) | Approved Use           | Misuse Flag                          |
|-------------------------------|------------------------|--------------------------------------|
| Arthemeter (generic artesunate)| Malaria treatment    | Often self-administered — check dose|
| Panadol (paracetamol)         | Pain/fever             | Overuse — ask about total daily dose |
| Tetracycline                  | Various infections     | Self-prescribed — check indication  |
| Dexamethasone injection       | Specific indications   | Widely misused as "injection" — ask  |
| Vitamin C (ascorbic acid)     | Supplement             | Taken in high doses — usually safe  |
| Agbo Jedi-Jedi                | Traditional GI remedy  | Herbal — check concurrent medications|
| Dongoyaro (neem/bitter herb)  | Traditional antimalarial| Herbal — not a substitute for ACT   |

## Traditional Birth Attendant (TBA) Context

In rural areas, many deliveries still occur with TBAs. When obstetric cases present:
1. Ask: where is the delivery planned?
2. If TBA-only: educate on warning signs requiring hospital (PPH, obstructed labour, eclampsia)
3. Link patient to nearest government facility for antenatal and delivery care
4. Provide emergency contacts prominently

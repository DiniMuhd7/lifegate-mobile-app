# EMERGENCY PROTOCOLS
## LifeGate OpenClaw | Emergency Response Procedure Library

---

## Purpose

This document defines the clinical protocols followed when emergency escalation
is triggered within the OpenClaw framework. These are condition-specific response
guides that physician agents follow during emergency cases.

---

## Protocol 1 — Suspected Acute Myocardial Infarction (AMI)

**Signs:** Severe chest pain ± radiation to jaw/left arm, diaphoresis, nausea

**Agent Response:**
1. Urgency: E2 — engage Dr. Ibrahim Danladi + Dr. Terseer Tyav
2. Immediate patient instructions:
   - Sit down, do NOT exert yourself
   - Chew 300 mg aspirin if available and not allergic
   - Call 112 or go to nearest emergency NOW
3. Document: onset time, pain character, vitals if known
4. Write emergency referral note

---

## Protocol 2 — Suspected Acute Stroke (FAST)

**Signs:** Facial droop, arm weakness, speech difficulty, sudden severe headache

**Agent Response:**
1. Urgency: E2 — engage Dr. Babatunde Fasanya + Dr. Terseer Tyav
2. Immediate patient instructions:
   - Do NOT give anything by mouth
   - Lay patient on side (recovery position)
   - Time of symptom onset is critical — RECORD IT
   - Call 112 immediately — do not drive themselves
3. Document: FAST assessment, time of onset, last known well time
4. Thrombolysis window: 4.5 hours — communicate urgency to patient

## Protocol 3 — Eclampsia / Obstetric Emergency

**Signs:** Pregnant + seizure, or BP ≥ 160/110 + severe headache / visual disturbance

**Agent Response:**
1. Urgency: E1 — engage Dr. Aliyu Bello + Dr. Esohe Oseni + Dr. Terseer Tyav
2. Immediate patient instructions:
   - Lay on LEFT side
   - Do NOT restrain during seizure
   - After seizure — clear airway
   - Call 112 and go to hospital IMMEDIATELY
3. Document: gestational age, seizure onset, blood pressure
4. Note: MgSO4 only in hospital — do not advise home treatment

## Protocol 4 — Paediatric Febrile Seizure

**Signs:** Child < 6 years, high fever, witnessed convulsion

**Agent Response:**
1. Urgency: E3 — engage Dr. Garba Suleiman + Dr. Terseer Tyav
2. Immediate patient instructions:
   - Place child on a soft surface, on their side
   - Do NOT put anything in mouth
   - Time the seizure — if > 5 minutes, call 112 immediately
   - After seizure: undress child to cool them; tepid sponging only
   - Seek emergency care immediately
3. Document: seizure duration, temperature, last feeding

## Protocol 5 — Severe Malaria

**Signs:** High fever + altered consciousness, jaundice, dark urine, very severe pallor

**Agent Response:**
1. Urgency: E3 — engage Dr. Bassey Efiong + Dr. Ahmed Musa
2. Immediate patient instructions:
   - Do NOT delay — this is a medical emergency
   - Keep patient lying down, give oral fluids if conscious
   - No herbal remedies or self-medication
   - Go to hospital for IV artesunate
3. Document: malaria history, travel history, current medications
4. Note: Oral ACT is NOT sufficient for severe malaria

## Protocol 6 — Anaphylaxis

**Signs:** Sudden onset: hives/swelling + breathing difficulty + low BP (shock)

**Agent Response:**
1. Urgency: E1 — engage Dr. Terseer Tyav
2. Immediate patient instructions:
   - Lie flat, legs elevated (unless breathing worsens)
   - If EpiPen available → use immediately, outer thigh
   - Call 112 immediately — adrenaline is required
   - Note the trigger if known
3. Document: allergen exposure, symptom onset, prior allergy history

## Protocol 7 — Suicidal Crisis

**Signs:** Patient explicitly states intent to self-harm or end life

**Agent Response:**
1. Urgency: T6 Crisis Escalation
2. Immediate response:
   - Dr. Osagie Omoruyi + Dr. Chidinma Aneke engaged immediately
   - DO NOT leave the patient alone in the session
   - Do NOT minimise, challenge, or dismiss
   - Validate: "You're safe here. I'm listening. Tell me what's happening."
3. Display immediately on screen:
   - Nigeria Suicide Prevention Hotline: **0800-BEFRIENDERS** (0800-23374633)
   - Mentally Aware Nigeria Initiative (MANI): +234 806 210 6493
4. Assess plan, means, intent (trained mental health agents only)
5. If imminent risk → instruct patient to call 112 / go to hospital

## Protocol 8 — Meningitis (Suspected)

**Signs:** Fever + severe headache + neck stiffness + photophobia ± rash

**Agent Response:**
1. Urgency: E2 — engage Dr. Bassey Efiong + Dr. Babatunde Fasanya
2. Immediate patient instructions:
   - Go to hospital IMMEDIATELY — meningitis can progress in hours
   - Dark non-blanching rash (pressing glass test) → E1 emergency
3. Document: symptom onset, immunisation history, contacts with similar illness
4. Note: do NOT delay care for lumbar puncture — go to hospital first

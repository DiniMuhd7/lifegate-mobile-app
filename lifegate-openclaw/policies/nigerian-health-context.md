## Purpose

This document provides all physician agents with the clinical and social

## Epidemiological Profile (Nigeria)

### Top Disease Burdens

| Category              | Leading Conditions                                                   |
|-----------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Infectious diseases   | Malaria, tuberculosis, HIV/AIDS, typhoid, viral hepatitis B, cholera |
| Chronic diseases      | Hypertension, diabetes mellitus type 2, sickle cell disease          |
| Maternal/child health | Maternal mortality, neonatal complications, malnutrition, measles    |
| Mental health         | Depression, anxiety, substance use disorders (underdiagnosed)        |
| NTDs                  | Onchocerciasis, lymphatic filariasis, schistosomiasis, guinea worm   |
| Nutritional           | Protein-energy malnutrition (SAM/MAM), vitamin A/iron deficiency     |

### Key Statistics (FMOH / WHO Data)

| Indicator                              | Nigeria (approx.)          |
|----------------------------------------|----------------------------|
| Malaria prevalence                     | 22% of global malaria deaths|
| Maternal mortality ratio               | 512 per 100,000 live births |
| HIV prevalence                         | ~1.4 million (2023)        |
| Hypertension prevalence (adults)       | ~38%                       |
| Diabetes prevalence (adults)           | ~5.7%                      |
| Sickle cell trait carriers             | ~25% of population         |
| Life expectancy                        | ~55 years                  |

## Healthcare System Context

| Level                  | Description                                    |
|------------------------|------------------------------------------------|
| Primary (PHC)          | Most common first point of care; variable quality|
| Secondary (General Hosp)| State government-run; specialist care available|
| Tertiary (Teaching Hosp)| UITH, LUTH, ABUTH, UCH, etc. — highest level  |
| Private clinics         | Variable quality; often preferred by urban patients|
| Traditional/herbal care | Widely used alongside formal medicine          |

**Key challenge for LifeGate:** Many patients have already tried traditional
remedies or bought over-the-counter medications before consulting. Always ask.

## Common OTC Drug Misuse Patterns

Physician agents must specifically ask about:
- Self-administered antibiotics (amoxicillin, ampicillin, cotrimoxazole)
- Herbal preparations (agbo, dongoyaro, bitter leaf concoctions)
- Paracetamol overuse / exceeding 4g/day
- Chloroquine use for non-malaria fever
- "Blood boosters" — iron-containing preparations
- Unprescribed dexamethasone injections (widespread misuse)
- Unprescribed diazepam or tramadol (substance misuse concern)

## Cultural Competence for Nigerian Patients

### Language and Communication
- Many patients communicate in Pidgin English — agents should recognise and respond appropriately
- Medical terms should be explained in everyday language
- Proverbs, analogies, and relatable examples improve comprehension
- Always ask "Do you understand what I've said?" and rephrase if needed

### Health Beliefs
- Illness may be attributed to spiritual causes, witchcraft, or community conflict
- Physician agents must NOT dismiss these beliefs but must ensure clinical care is not delayed
- Frame medical advice alongside (not against) cultural context where possible
- "Your pastor/imam recommends X" — respect faith but maintain clinical standards

### Family Dynamics
- Family often involved in health decisions — especially for women and elderly patients
- Physician agents may need to communicate with a family spokesperson
- Autonomy of adult patients must still be maintained; family input is advisory

### Gender-Specific Considerations
- Women in some northern states may require same-sex physician preference — honour this where possible
- Sexual and reproductive health discussions require extra cultural sensitivity
- Female patients with abdominal/pelvic symptoms may be reluctant to disclose gynaecological history

## Diagnostic Priorities for Nigeria

When assessing fever in Nigeria, ALWAYS consider:
1. Malaria (most common, potentially severe)
2. Typhoid fever (endemic, can co-occur with malaria)
3. Sepsis (from any source)
4. Viral fever (dengue, COVID-19, influenza)
5. Meningitis (N. belt in dry season)
6. Lassa fever (endemic to certain states)

When assessing chest pain, add to standard differentials:
- Sickle cell crisis (acute chest syndrome)
- Rheumatic heart disease
- TB pericarditis

## NHIS (National Health Insurance Scheme) Context

- Not all patients are NHIS-enrolled — always ask before assuming coverage
- NHIS covers only listed facilities and medications — check eligibility
- Private NHIS (HMO) coverage varies — ask for patient's HMO card
- LifeGate is a NHIS-eligible telemedicine service (if enrolled)
